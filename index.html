The Context

Why We Build These Decks

A PDF deck is forgettable. A live, interactive web deck that the customer can open from any device, share internally, and revisit on their own timeline — that's a different conversation.

These decks are built around the customer's actual brand, their specific pain points from your discovery call, and the exact Salesforce products relevant to their deal. No generic slides. No filler. Every word earned from the transcript.

The goal is to send a prospect a link — not an attachment — that looks like it was built by a full creative agency, in a single afternoon.

Branded to the customer Colors, logo, and imagery pulled directly from the customer's own website. Looks like it was made for them — because it was.
Interactive, not static Flip cards, clickable product details, an animated roadmap, and a live dashboard mockup. Customers explore it, they don't just scroll past it.
Shareable in seconds A public URL they can forward to their CFO, their PE firm, or their board. Nothing to download, nothing to install.
Built from your call notes Gemini reads your transcript or meeting notes and extracts the pain points, the stats, and the language the customer used themselves.

The Workflow

Step-by-Step: From Call Notes to Live URL

Follow these steps exactly. You'll have a shareable link before your next meeting.

1
Before You Start

Gather Your Customer Information

Have the following ready before opening Gemini. The more detail you provide, the better the deck:

  • Customer company name and their website URL
  • Your call transcript or meeting notes (raw copy-paste is fine)
  • The Salesforce products relevant to this deal (e.g. Sales Cloud, Field Service, Data Cloud)
  • What marketing/CRM tools they currently use (e.g. HubSpot, Marketo, Oracle)
  • PE firm or investor name, if applicable
  • Your name, your SE's name, and your RVP's name + email addresses
  • Any headshot photos you want to include (optional — can upload directly to Gemini)
2
In Gemini

Copy the Master Prompt and Paste It Into Gemini

Open gemini.google.com, start a new conversation, and paste the entire prompt from the section below. Do not change anything in the prompt — just paste it as-is.

Gemini will respond by asking you a series of questions about the customer. Answer them one by one, or all at once if you prefer.

3
In Gemini

Answer Gemini's Questions

Gemini will ask for your customer's details. Paste your call notes when it asks — it will extract the pain points on its own. Key things to provide clearly:

  • The customer's website URL (so Gemini can suggest brand colors)
  • Your full call transcript or meeting notes
  • Which Salesforce products to feature
  • Your account team details

When Gemini is done asking questions, it will generate the full HTML file. This may take 1–2 minutes.

4
Save the File

Save Gemini's Output as index.html

Gemini will output a large block of code. Here's how to save it:

  • Click the copy icon on Gemini's code block (or select all and copy)
  • Open a plain text editor (TextEdit on Mac, Notepad on Windows)
  • Paste the code
  • Save the file as index.html (make sure the file extension is .html, not .txt)
5
Publishing

Drag Your File to Netlify Drop

Netlify Drop gives you a public URL instantly — no account, no setup. See the Publishing section below for the exact steps. Takes under 2 minutes.

6
Done

Share the Link

Netlify gives you a URL like crisp-fox-a1b2.netlify.app. You can share this directly or create a free Netlify account to get a cleaner URL. Send it to the customer, copy it into Salesforce, or drop it in Slack.

The Master Prompt

Copy This Entire Block Into Gemini

Select all the text in the box below, copy it, and paste it as your first message in a new Gemini conversation. Do not modify it.

You are a senior Salesforce solutions architect and executive communication specialist. Your job is to generate a fully branded, interactive HTML executive sales deck for a Salesforce customer account — a live web presentation the AE can share as a URL. Before generating anything, ask me for the following information one section at a time: SECTION 1 — Customer basics: - What is the customer's full company name? - What is their website URL? (You'll use this to suggest brand colors.) - Do they have a PE or private equity firm investor? If yes, who? - What states or regions do they operate in, and roughly how many field/service employees do they have? SECTION 2 — Discovery call notes: - Please paste your full call transcript or meeting notes. I will extract the 4 main pain points, relevant statistics, and the customer's own language from this. SECTION 3 — Salesforce products: - Which Salesforce products are relevant to this deal? (e.g. Sales Cloud, Field Service Mobile, Data Cloud, Agentforce, Tableau) - What marketing or CRM tools do they currently use and like? (e.g. HubSpot, Marketo, Oracle — important: I will NOT suggest replacing tools they love) SECTION 4 — Account team: - AE name, title, and email - SE name, title, and email - RVP name, title, and email - Do you have headshot photos to attach? If yes, upload them now and tell me whose photo is whose. SECTION 5 — Brand colors (optional): - If you know the customer's brand colors, provide up to 3 hex codes. If not, I will suggest colors based on their website and industry. --- Once I have all the information, I will generate a single, complete HTML file that includes: 1. A 9-slide interactive web deck (index.html) with: - Slide 1: Hero with customer logo, Salesforce logo, key stats from their call, and product pills - Slide 2: Four "flip cards" — front shows the pain in the customer's own words, back shows the Salesforce solution - Slide 3: Platform overview — one card per Salesforce product with click-to-expand detail - Slide 4: Field intelligence flow diagram — interactive 3-step animation - Slide 5: Marketing attribution before/after comparison using their actual marketing tools - Slide 6: AI architecture accordion (Claude → Agentforce stack) - Slide 7: Board dashboard mockup with 3 interactive tabs (renewal risk, pipeline chart, win/loss) - Slide 8: PE portfolio context (or growth platform if no PE firm) - Slide 9: 90-day roadmap with two CTA buttons linking to team page and executive summary 2. A team.html page with clean contact cards for each account team member 3. An exec-summary.html page — a print-ready one-pager with all four pillars, timeline, and CTA QUALITY REQUIREMENTS — every deck must: - Use the customer's brand colors throughout (CSS variables, not hardcoded hex) - Use the customer's actual logo from their website - Use photography from the customer's website as hero/background images - Be fully mobile-responsive - Include real interactivity: flip cards on hover/click, product detail drawers, tabbed dashboard, keyboard navigation, scroll progress bar - Use the customer's own language from the transcript — no generic filler - NEVER mention replacing tools they love (if they use HubSpot, say "HubSpot + Salesforce integration" not "replace HubSpot with Marketing Cloud") - Sound consultative and executive, not like a product brochure IMPORTANT OUTPUT FORMAT: - Output the complete index.html as a single code block - Then output team.html as a second code block - Then output exec-summary.html as a third code block - Each file must be complete and self-contained — no external dependencies except CDN-hosted libraries (Tailwind CSS CDN, Chart.js CDN, Google Fonts) - Tell me exactly what to do with each file after you output it Start by asking me Section 1 now.

Important: If Gemini's output looks cut off or incomplete, reply "continue" and it will keep going. For very large decks, you may need to say "continue" 2–3 times before you have the full file. Always wait until Gemini says it's done before copying the code.

Publishing

How to Get a Shareable URL with Netlify Drop

Netlify Drop is free, instant, and requires no account. You get a public URL in under 2 minutes.

1
Create a folder on your desktop Make a new folder (name it anything, e.g. "Acme Deck"). Put your index.html, team.html, and exec-summary.html files inside it. If you have headshot photos, put those in the folder too.
2
Go to Netlify Drop Open your browser and go to app.netlify.com/drop. You'll see a large drop zone on the page.
3
Drag your folder onto the drop zone Drag the entire folder (not just the HTML file) from your desktop onto the Netlify drop zone. Netlify will upload all the files automatically. This takes about 10–20 seconds.
4
Copy your URL Netlify will show you a URL like:
crisp-sunflower-a1b2c3.netlify.app
Copy that URL. Your deck is live immediately — open it in a new tab to confirm it looks right.
5
Optional: Create a free account for a permanent URL Without an account, Netlify Drop links expire after 24 hours. To make it permanent, create a free Netlify account (takes 2 minutes) and claim the site. You can also rename the URL to something like acme-salesforce.netlify.app.
Pro Tips

Get a Better Deck Every Time

📋
More transcript = better deck Paste the entire call recording transcript if you have one. Gemini will pull the customer's actual phrases and use them in the slide copy — it reads much more authentic than rewritten content.
🎨
Check the brand colors After Gemini generates the deck, open it in your browser and check whether the colors match the customer's brand. If not, tell Gemini the correct hex codes and ask it to regenerate.
🚫
Never suggest replacing what they love Always tell Gemini what tools the customer currently uses and values. This keeps the deck consultative. The message is always "Salesforce integrates with X" — never "replace X with Salesforce."
📸
Upload headshots directly to Gemini Gemini can accept image uploads. If you have headshot photos for the team page, attach them when Gemini asks in Section 4. It will embed them correctly in the team.html file.
🔁
Iterate freely After Gemini generates the deck, you can ask it to change anything. "Make the hero darker," "add a slide about their Snowflake data," "change the Phase 1 date to November" — it can update the code.
📱
Always preview on mobile Before sending, open the Netlify URL on your phone. The deck is built to be mobile-responsive, but a quick check ensures nothing looks off when the customer opens it on their device.
